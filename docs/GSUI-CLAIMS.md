# GSUI CLAIMS

## Core Claims, Working Hypotheses, and Open Questions

**General Structure Unfolding Intelligence (GSUI)**

---

## Purpose of This Document

General Structure Unfolding Intelligence is intentionally broad.

It connects:

```text id="clm001"
Folding
Structure
Unfolding
Core + Delta
Unfolding Space
Control Plane
Invariant
Certification
Growth
Structural Self
World Models
Machine-Native Perception
```

A framework with this scope can easily be misunderstood if all statements are presented with the same level of certainty.

This document therefore separates GSUI propositions into three categories:

```text id="clm002"
CORE CLAIMS

WORKING HYPOTHESES

OPEN QUESTIONS
```

The purpose is methodological discipline.

GSUI should distinguish:

> what is structurally direct,

from:

> what is a plausible theoretical extension,

from:

> what remains genuinely unresolved.

---

# 1. Claim Levels

## Level A — Core Claims

These are the strongest claims currently made by GSUI.

They are intended to be:

* conceptually direct;
* structurally defensible;
* compatible with existing computational systems;
* useful without requiring acceptance of the full GSUI theory.

They do not imply that GSUI has already been experimentally validated as a complete general theory of intelligence.

---

## Level B — Working Hypotheses

These are broader theoretical propositions.

They appear promising within the GSUI framework but require:

```text id="clm003"
formalization,
comparison,
implementation,
experimentation,
or cross-domain validation.
```

They should not be presented as established facts.

---

## Level C — Open Questions

These are unresolved research problems.

They identify areas where GSUI currently provides:

```text id="clm004"
a vocabulary,
a structural question,
or a possible direction,
```

but not a settled answer.

---

# 2. Core Claim A1 — Structure Formation Is Not the Whole Intelligence Problem

A large portion of AI research concerns how structure is formed.

Examples include:

```text id="clm005"
Training
Compression
Representation Learning
Graph Extraction
Tree Construction
Memory Formation
Model Learning
```

GSUI calls this broad direction **Folding**.

But after structure has formed, another problem remains:

> **How does that structure become runtime behavior?**

Therefore:

> **Structure formation is only one part of the intelligence problem; runtime structural unfolding deserves independent study.**

This is a foundational GSUI claim.

---

# 3. Core Claim A2 — Persistent Structure Can Constrain Runtime Behavior

A previously formed structure can constrain or enable future transitions.

Examples include:

```text id="clm006"
LLM parameters
CallingGraphs
Decision trees
Policy structures
ANN topology and weights
World models
Memory systems
Vehicle dynamics
```

Therefore:

> **Existing structure can shape what becomes reachable, likely, permitted, or executable at runtime.**

This claim does not depend on any one implementation.

---

# 4. Core Claim A3 — Core + Delta Is a Useful Structural Form

GSUI uses the minimal representation:

```text id="clm007"
<Core Structure> +/- <Delta Modification>
```

or:

$$
S_{t+1}=S_t\pm\Delta_t
$$

This should not be interpreted as literal arithmetic in every domain.

It means:

> **a structural transition can often be described relative to an existing reference structure and a modification of that structure or state.**

This is a comparative structural form.

It is not claimed to be a complete mathematical theory.

---

# 5. Core Claim A4 — Delta Is Broader Than Addition

A Delta may include:

```text id="clm008"
Add
Remove
Replace
Branch
Merge
Reconnect
Reweight
Activate
Suppress
Select

```

Therefore:

> **Structural unfolding is not equivalent to structural expansion.**

A useful intelligent transition may reduce, suppress, preserve, or reorganize structure.

# 6. Core Claim A5 — Generation Is One Form of Unfolding

Generation produces an artifact or continuation.

Examples include:

```text id="clm009"
Text
Code
Image
Plan
Trajectory
```

But unfolding may also involve:

```text id="clm010"
branch selection,
suppression,
movement,
routing,
reconfiguration,
preservation,
structural growth.
```

Therefore, conceptually:

$$
Generation \subset Unfolding
$$

within the GSUI vocabulary.

This is a classification claim, not a mathematical set-theoretic theorem.

---

# 7. Core Claim A6 — Context Matters to Unfolding

The same structure can behave differently under different runtime conditions.

For example:

```text id="clm011"
Same LLM
+ different prompt
→ different continuation

Same CallingGraph
+ different task
→ different structural modification

Same vehicle
+ different road condition
→ different safe trajectory
```

Therefore:

> **Unfolding is generally context-conditioned.**

---

# 8. Core Claim A7 — Triggering Is Distinct from Stored Capability

A system may contain latent capability without continuously exercising it.

A Trigger may localize or activate a relevant region of that capability.

Examples include:

```text id="clm012"
Prompt
Function call
Sensor event
Threshold crossing
Task request
Detected structural gap
```

Therefore:

> **Stored structural capacity and runtime triggering should be analytically distinguished.**

---

# 9. Core Claim A8 — Possible, Allowed, Preferred, and Selected Are Different

A system may be capable of a transition without being authorized to perform it.

GSUI therefore distinguishes:

$$
\mathcal{U}_{possible}
$$

$$
\mathcal{U}_{reachable}
$$

$$
\mathcal{U}_{allowed}
$$

$$
\mathcal{U}_{preferred}
$$

$$
\mathcal{U}_{selected}
$$

and potentially:

$$
\mathcal{U}_{certified}
$$

Thus:

> **Capability, authorization, preference, selection, and certification should not be collapsed into one concept.**

This is especially important for autonomous systems.

---

# 10. Core Claim A9 — Control Is Part of Runtime Intelligence

If a system must determine:

```text id="clm013"
what becomes active,
what is excluded,
what is allowed,
what is preferred,
what is certified,
```

then control mechanisms directly affect runtime intelligence.

Therefore:

> **Constraint, policy, evaluation, and certification can be part of the intelligence architecture rather than merely external additions.**

This does not imply that all intelligence must contain an explicit symbolic Control Plane.

---

# 11. Core Claim A10 — Evaluation and Certification Are Different

Evaluation asks:

> How desirable is this candidate?

Certification asks:

> Is this candidate acceptable for consequential execution?

A high-scoring candidate may still violate:

```text id="clm014"
security,
safety,
policy,
architecture,
invariants,
permissions.
```

Therefore:

> **Optimization or evaluation alone does not automatically imply authorization.**

---

# 12. Core Claim A11 — Invariants Matter Across Structural Change

A changing system may still need to preserve critical properties.

Therefore:

> **Structural change and structural continuity must be studied together.**

An invariant may encode:

```text id="clm015"
Safety
Compatibility
Identity continuity
Architecture
Control stability
Security
Functional requirements
```

This leads to:

> **Invariant checking is one possible mechanism for determining acceptable continuity across unfolding.**

---

# 13. Core Claim A12 — Search and Structural Unfolding Are Not Identical

Search commonly operates inside a candidate space that is sufficiently defined for the search process.

Structural unfolding may modify the structure that generates future possibilities.

Therefore:

> **Search can select within a space, while structural unfolding can alter the future space itself.**

If:

$$
S_t\rightarrow S_{t+1}
$$

then:

$$
\mathcal{U}(S_t)
\neq
\mathcal{U}(S_{t+1})
$$

may hold.

This is one of the central GSUI distinctions.

---

# 14. Core Claim A13 — Feedback Is Not Automatically Learning

A system can receive feedback without retaining structural change.

Therefore:

$$
Feedback\neq Growth
$$

in general.

GSUI reserves **Structural Growth** for cases where feedback contributes to persistent structure such that future unfolding is affected.

Thus:

> **Persistent change, not feedback alone, is the key structural criterion for growth.**

---

# 15. Core Claim A14 — Structural Growth Changes Future Unfolding

If retained structural change modifies what the system can later generate, reach, select, or activate, then future Unfolding Space has changed.

Therefore:

> **Structural Growth changes future unfolding capacity.**

This may involve:

```text id="clm016"
Expansion
Contraction
Reorganization
Direct routing
New capability
New constraint
New invariant
```

Growth is therefore not synonymous with simple size increase.

---

# 16. Core Claim A15 — Machine Perception Need Not Be Human-Like

Machines may receive and organize signals that have no direct human sensory equivalent.

Examples include:

```text id="clm017"
CallingGraph topology
Network latency
RF spectrum
Database contention
Runtime traces
Machine telemetry
Memory pressure
Dependency structure
```

Therefore:

> **Human sensory categories are not required as the universal template for machine perception.**

This claim concerns operational perception, not phenomenal experience.

---

# 17. Core Claim A16 — Machine-Native Cognition Does Not Eliminate Governance

A machine may use internal representations that are difficult for humans to interpret directly.

This does not imply unrestricted external action.

Therefore:

$$
InternalRepresentationFreedom
\neq
ExternalActionFreedom
$$

GSUI maintains:

> **Machine-native cognition and human-governable consequences are compatible.**

---

# 18. Core Claim A17 — Internal Interpretability, Structural Observability, and Action Certifiability Are Distinct

These three goals are different.

### Internal Interpretability

Understanding internal computation.

### Structural Observability

Identifying consequential proposed structural change.

### Action Certifiability

Determining whether that change satisfies required conditions.

Therefore:

> **Limited internal interpretability does not logically require abandoning structural observability or certification.**

This distinction may become increasingly important for advanced AI systems.

---

# 19. Core Claim A18 — World-Model Meaning Can Be Self-Relative

The same objective world condition may imply different operational possibilities for different systems.

For example:

```text id="clm018"
Same corridor

small robot:
passable

large vehicle:
blocked
```

Therefore:

> **For action-oriented intelligence, some world-model semantics depend on the capabilities and constraints of the acting system.**

This does not imply that all objective world facts are subjective or self-relative.

---

# 20. Core Claim A19 — Structural Self Is a Useful Operational Reference Concept

A Delta requires a reference structure.

Capability, continuity, growth, and Unfolding Space also frequently require an operational subject.

GSUI therefore introduces:

> **Structural Self as the persistent structural reference frame relative to which unfolding, modification, capability, preservation, and continuity are defined.**

This is currently an operational definition.

It is not a consciousness claim.

---

# 21. Core Claim A20 — Structural Self Does Not Imply Consciousness

GSUI explicitly separates:

```text id="clm019"
Structural Self
Operational Self
Cognitive Self
Narrative / Human Self
```

Therefore:

> **The presence of a Structural Self does not establish consciousness, subjective experience, personhood, or moral status.**

This boundary is part of the GSUI framework.

---

# 22. Core Claim A21 — Capability and Permission Are Distinct

An AI may technically possess the capability to perform an action without having permission to do so.

Thus:

$$
Capability\neq Permission
$$

and:

$$
PossibleUnfolding\neq AuthorizedUnfolding
$$

This distinction is essential to controlled autonomous systems.

---

# 23. Core Claim A22 — Greater Capability Can Increase the Need for Control

As a system gains more tools, memory, autonomy, or structural modification ability, its consequential action space may expand.

Therefore:

> **Increasing internal or operational capability can increase the importance of structural governance at consequential boundaries.**

A compact GSUI principle is:

> **The more freedom an AI has in internal unfolding, the stronger the certification requirement may need to become at consequential external boundaries.**

```text id="clm021"

Coding
Control
```

but does not claim that every intelligent process must fit GSUI equally well.

The hypothesis should be evaluated domain by domain.

# 28. Working Hypothesis B4 — Scaling Enlarges Unfolding Potential

Large learned models often support richer context-sensitive behavior.

GSUI proposes a structural interpretation:

> **Scaling may enlarge the latent space of possible unfolding.**

A compact expression is:

> **Scaling enlarges unfolding potential; structural intelligence governs unfolding.**

This should be treated as an interpretive hypothesis rather than a settled causal law.

# 29. Working Hypothesis B5 — Expert Intelligence Often Compresses Effective Unfolding Space

Expert systems and skilled organisms often avoid large amounts of unnecessary search.

GSUI hypothesizes that expertise may frequently involve:

```text id="clm024"
better triggering,
better localization,
direct routing,
stronger pruning,
better structural reuse.
```

Thus:

> **Intelligence may improve partly by reducing the effective space that must be explored.**

This is compatible with, but not identical to, traditional search-efficiency explanations.

# 30. Working Hypothesis B6 — Structural Growth Can Be More Important Than Skill Accumulation

Adding more skills does not necessarily reorganize intelligence.

Structural Growth may instead modify:

```text id="clm025"

triggering,
control,
future reachability.
```

Therefore GSUI hypothesizes:

> **Structural reorganization may produce larger long-term intelligence gains than simple accumulation of independent skills.**

This requires empirical investigation.

WM(Self,World,\Delta)

This is a working hypothesis, not a universal definition of world models.

# 34. Working Hypothesis B10 — Invariants Can Help Formalize Structural Identity

If a system changes continuously, some preserved properties may help determine continuity.

GSUI hypothesizes:

> **Invariant-preserving transitions may provide one useful basis for formalizing Structural Self continuity across change.**

* different systems may use different continuity criteria;
* local invariants may be insufficient for long-horizon identity.

# 35. Working Hypothesis B11 — Structural Certification Can Bridge Opaque Cognition and Governable Action

Advanced AI systems may become increasingly difficult to interpret internally.

GSUI proposes:

CallingGraph certification for AI coding is one example.

Whether analogous structures can be found in other domains remains an open research program.

```text id="clm028"
its policy,
its evaluation,
its future possibility space.
```

GSUI hypothesizes:

> **Autonomous structural intelligence becomes qualitatively more powerful when it can modify the mechanisms that define its own future unfolding.**

This introduces Meta-Unfolding.
